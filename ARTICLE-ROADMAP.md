# Truth to Live By — article roadmap

Updated 27 September 2026. This is the complete first-season editorial plan: 30 substantive articles, ten per pillar. Titles and publication order remain working editorial decisions until owner review.

Nothing in this roadmap grants publication approval. Planned articles must be researched, drafted, reviewed and explicitly approved before `draft` or `placeholder` flags are removed. Draft dates are not public publication dates.

## Editorial purpose

Each article should answer the brand's central question: **What is the truth here that somebody could actually live by?**

The slate converts recurring short-form demand signals into durable subjects rather than chasing individual memes. The research clusters were:

- discipline, habits and harsh self-improvement;
- social uncertainty and fear of judgment;
- friendship maintenance, loneliness and relationship interpretation;
- sleep, attention and bedtime procrastination;
- health tracking, health anxiety and frightening claims;
- memory, identity, nostalgia and online personality labels.

The main research source for demand and format signals was [Social Growth Engineers](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/). Its reported views and case studies are treated as marketing signals, not independent scientific evidence. Each factual article requires its own primary or authoritative sources.

## Publication phases

### Phase 1 — launch library

Articles 1–6 provide two substantive pieces per pillar. Articles 1–3 have been drafted and remain unpublished placeholders. Articles 4–6 are the next planned article batch.

### Phase 2 — core library

Articles 7–18 establish the publication's recurring subjects. This phase includes full redevelopment of the four original sample essays.

### Phase 3 — authority library

Articles 19–30 cover more nuanced or evidence-intensive territory after the editorial voice and review process are established.

## Self

### 1. Discipline Is Not Self-Punishment

**Purpose:** Distinguish consistency, constraint and commitment from shame, deprivation and self-contempt. Show that useful discipline often reduces repeated internal conflict by changing habits and conditions.

**Reader outcome:** A firm but revisable discipline plan with a reason, a clear cue and a recovery rule.

**Evidence:** Motivation, self-determination, self-compassion, beneficial habits and implementation intentions.

**Status:** Full draft exists at `src/content/articles/discipline-is-not-self-punishment.md`.

### 4. Nobody Cares—and That Can Set You Free

**Purpose:** Use the “Nobody Really Cares” trend to examine the spotlight effect and the tendency to overestimate how closely others notice our mistakes and presentation.

**Reader outcome:** One small exposure or expression chosen without trying to control every observer's impression.

**Evidence:** Spotlight-effect experiments, social-evaluative anxiety and impression management. The article must balance liberation with genuine social responsibility.

### 7. What Deserves Your Attention?

**Purpose:** Ask what should receive a finite life before asking how to become more efficient. Develop the existing reflection into a sourced anchor article.

**Reader outcome:** A practical attention audit and one protected occasion for something the reader values.

**Evidence:** Attention, interruption, task switching and digital-environment research.

**Status:** The current sample remains unpublished and must be substantially expanded.

### 10. Why Your Days Disappear From Memory

**Purpose:** Explain how attention, routine, encoding, stress and sleep can contribute to the ordinary feeling that a day left few retrievable memories.

**Reader outcome:** A non-diagnostic way to create and notice more distinct moments.

**Evidence:** Memory encoding, event segmentation, attentional load, stress and sleep. Include a medical boundary for concerning memory changes.

### 13. Letting Go Is Not the Same as Giving Up

**Purpose:** Distinguish acceptance, avoidance and resignation. Ask what remains ours to influence and what becomes costly to keep fighting.

**Reader outcome:** A two-column examination of what can be acted on and what must currently be carried or released.

**Evidence:** Stoic and other philosophical primary texts plus acceptance and coping research. Interpretive claims must remain labelled as such.

### 16. Not Every Question Needs an Answer Today

**Purpose:** Separate a real decision deadline from the emotional urge to settle an unknowable future.

**Reader outcome:** A method for identifying what needs action now, what information is missing and what can responsibly remain open.

**Evidence:** Intolerance of uncertainty, decision-making and rumination.

**Status:** The current sample remains unpublished and must be substantially expanded.

### 19. Are You a Personality Type—or Just Playing One Online?

**Purpose:** Explore why personality labels, archetypes and AI-generated identity readings feel meaningful, while showing how labels can narrow identity through confirmation and performance.

**Reader outcome:** A way to use a label as a question rather than a verdict.

**Evidence:** Personality measurement, the Barnum effect, self-concept and online identity.

### 22. Nostalgia Is Not a Plan—but It Tells You What You Miss

**Purpose:** Treat nostalgia as information about belonging, continuity or neglected values, not proof that the past was objectively better.

**Reader outcome:** Identify the quality being missed and recreate a proportionate part of it in the present.

**Evidence:** Autobiographical memory, rosy retrospection and nostalgia research.

### 25. The Nervous System Is Not a Personality

**Purpose:** Clarify what emotional regulation can reasonably mean and challenge social content that labels every difficult feeling as dysregulation, trauma or a fixed nervous-system type.

**Reader outcome:** More precise language for distress and a clearer boundary between ordinary difficulty and conditions needing professional care.

**Evidence:** Stress physiology, emotion regulation and trauma terminology. Expert review is desirable before publication.

### 28. You Do Not Need a Grand Purpose to Choose a Direction

**Purpose:** Challenge the idea that action must wait for discovery of a single life purpose. Explore meaning as something that may emerge through commitments, relationships and contribution.

**Reader outcome:** Choose a direction worth testing without pretending it settles an entire life.

**Evidence:** Philosophy of meaning, purpose and wellbeing research.

## Relationships

### 2. Why Adult Friendships Need Rituals, Not Just Good Intentions

**Purpose:** Explain how small repeated acts can sustain friendship after school, proximity and shared institutions stop creating automatic contact.

**Reader outcome:** A recurring, flexible ritual with a chosen friend and an agreed way to return after interruptions.

**Evidence:** Friendship maintenance, interaction, shared activity, autonomy support and adult wellbeing.

**Status:** Full draft exists at `src/content/articles/why-adult-friendships-need-rituals.md`.

### 5. Why “Can We Talk?” Feels Like a Threat

**Purpose:** Begin with the ambiguous message that makes the mind invent a disaster. Examine uncertainty, social threat and the interpretations constructed before any facts arrive.

**Reader outcome:** A short process for separating the message received from the story added to it.

**Evidence:** Anticipatory anxiety, ambiguity, rejection sensitivity and threat appraisal. Avoid popular attachment-style diagnosis.

### 8. The Space Between Listening and Replying

**Purpose:** Examine listening for meaning before solving, correcting or sharing a similar story. Preserve boundaries: listening is not agreement or unlimited availability.

**Reader outcome:** Ask one clarifying question before offering a solution in an ordinary safe conversation.

**Evidence:** Active listening, perceived responsiveness and supportive communication.

**Status:** The current sample remains unpublished and must be substantially expanded.

### 11. Red Flags, Gut Feelings and Internet Relationship Advice

**Purpose:** Explain why warning lists spread and distinguish harmful patterns from preferences, isolated mistakes, anxiety and algorithmically amplified suspicion.

**Reader outcome:** A framework based on patterns, impact, repair and safety rather than viral labels.

**Evidence:** Confirmation bias, risk perception, trust and relationship safety. The article must not minimise coercion or abuse.

### 14. What Repair Looks Like After an Argument

**Purpose:** Focus less on avoiding every conflict and more on accountability, accurate apology, restitution, changed behaviour and rebuilding trust.

**Reader outcome:** Identify the repair actually required rather than merely trying to end discomfort.

**Evidence:** Conflict repair, apology and relationship research. Do not imply that every relationship can or should be repaired.

### 17. Why Loneliness Makes Reaching Out Harder

**Purpose:** Explain the loop in which loneliness can increase vigilance and discourage the approaches that might create connection.

**Reader outcome:** One low-pressure act that creates possibility without demanding immediate intimacy.

**Evidence:** Loneliness, rejection expectations, social withdrawal and community interventions.

### 20. Different People, Same Friendship

**Purpose:** Use the “different aesthetics, same friendship” signal to examine how much similarity friendship really requires.

**Reader outcome:** Identify the shared values or practices underneath visible difference.

**Evidence:** Homophily, similarity, complementarity and friendship formation.

### 23. The Story You Tell Yourself When Someone Does Not Reply

**Purpose:** Explore the meanings assigned to silence: rejection, anger, disinterest, danger or simple overload.

**Reader outcome:** Separate observable facts, communication norms and imagined explanations before acting.

**Evidence:** Attribution bias, digital communication and relational uncertainty.

### 26. The Family Role You Never Chose

**Purpose:** Examine roles such as peacemaker, achiever, responsible one or difficult one, and ask when an old adaptation becomes an adult identity that can be renegotiated.

**Reader outcome:** Name a recurring role and choose one small interaction in which not to perform it automatically.

**Evidence:** Family-systems theory and empirical family research. Popular role labels must not be presented as diagnoses.

### 29. Love Without Losing Your Separate Life

**Purpose:** Explore interdependence: intimacy that allows friendship, solitude, work and independent interests to survive.

**Reader outcome:** Identify one independent commitment and one shared commitment worth protecting.

**Evidence:** Autonomy support, intimacy, boundaries and relationship satisfaction.

## Living Well

### 3. Why You Stay Up Even When You’re Exhausted

**Purpose:** Examine bedtime procrastination as more than a simple discipline failure, including depleted control, delayed leisure, social connection and the wish to reclaim unscripted time.

**Reader outcome:** Identify what the late night is providing, meet some of that need earlier and give bedtime a visible beginning.

**Evidence:** Sleep-duration guidance, bedtime procrastination, planning, autonomy and digital leisure.

**Status:** Full draft exists at `src/content/articles/why-you-stay-up-even-when-youre-exhausted.md`.

### 6. When Tracking Helps—and When It Starts Owning You

**Purpose:** Examine sleep, calorie, skin, exercise and habit tracking. Show when measurement supports awareness and when the metric displaces the purpose.

**Reader outcome:** Decide what the metric is for, how often it deserves attention and what would justify pausing it.

**Evidence:** Self-monitoring, quantified-self research, behavioural feedback and risks associated with obsessive tracking. High evidence burden.

### 9. A Small Promise You Can Keep

**Purpose:** Explain why specificity and environmental support often help more than grand identity declarations.

**Reader outcome:** One small, situated action and a compassionate return rule.

**Evidence:** Habit formation, context cues, implementation intentions and lapse recovery. Reject universal fixed-duration habit claims.

**Status:** The current sample remains unpublished and must be substantially expanded.

### 12. Health Advice Designed to Frighten You

**Purpose:** Investigate “this is why you’re sick” hooks, shocking comparisons and frightening certainty. Give readers a method for assessing claims without requiring scientific expertise.

**Reader outcome:** A compact check covering source, population, outcome, absolute effect and uncertainty.

**Evidence:** Health misinformation, fear appeals, risk communication and evidence hierarchies. Use only verified examples.

### 15. When Streaks Help—and When They Turn Against You

**Purpose:** Explain why visible progress motivates and why one missed day can turn a useful practice into perceived failure.

**Reader outcome:** Replace perfect streaks with return rate, flexible consistency or another metric tied to the real purpose.

**Evidence:** Gamification, intrinsic motivation, goal gradients and lapse recovery.

### 18. Why Rest Does Not Feel Restful Anymore

**Purpose:** Distinguish genuine recovery from passive overstimulation without reducing the subject to “screens are bad.”

**Reader outcome:** Compare one period of accidental leisure with one period of deliberately chosen rest.

**Evidence:** Recovery, attentional residue, leisure quality and digital media use.

### 21. The Best Workout Is the One You Can Return To

**Purpose:** Counter workout hacks and dramatic transformations with adherence, enjoyment, progression and circumstances.

**Reader outcome:** Define a realistic minimum and a safe way to progress.

**Evidence:** Exercise adherence, public-health guidance and injury-safe boundaries. This is not an individual exercise prescription.

### 24. Busy Is Not the Same as Useful

**Purpose:** Examine busyness as identity, reassurance and avoidance. Distinguish maintenance work, meaningful progress and activity performed mainly to feel in control.

**Reader outcome:** Review one week by contribution and necessity rather than volume alone.

**Evidence:** Busyness bias, productivity, task switching and occupational wellbeing.

### 27. Why Money Shame Makes Better Decisions Harder

**Purpose:** Explore the moral meanings attached to bank balances, spending and financial mistakes while acknowledging real material constraints.

**Reader outcome:** Separate the financial fact, emotional judgment and next decision.

**Evidence:** Behavioural finance, scarcity, shame and decision-making. Explicitly not financial advice.

### 30. Longevity Advice Has an Uncertainty Problem

**Purpose:** Explain how confident longevity promises can outrun findings based on surrogate outcomes, observational associations, animal studies or early trials.

**Reader outcome:** Assess the maturity of evidence before adopting a health claim.

**Evidence:** Primary health sources, systematic reviews, absolute risk, replication and research limitations. This has the highest editorial and sourcing burden in the slate.

## Standard article structure

Every article should normally contain:

1. a specific lived moment;
2. the question underneath it;
3. what people commonly claim;
4. what credible evidence suggests;
5. what remains uncertain or contextual;
6. the practical Truth to Live By;
7. one proportionate experiment or action;
8. an evidence note and classified sources;
9. two or three related articles.

Health, exercise, sleep, anxiety, memory and longevity articles need the strongest evidence notes. Philosophical essays should use primary texts when attributing ideas and must not borrow scientific authority for interpretive claims.

## Editorial and release gates

- Every factual claim must be traceable to a suitable source.
- Sources must distinguish established evidence, emerging evidence, interpretation, opinion and hypothesis.
- Health and psychology pieces must describe important limitations and avoid diagnosis.
- Local image rights and meaningful alt text must be confirmed.
- Owner editorial approval must be recorded before draft and placeholder flags are removed.
- The actual publication date must replace any provisional draft date at approval.
- Production isolation, links, related references, accessibility and layout must pass the required validation suite.
- The release minimum and approval flags must never be reduced to make an incomplete library appear ready.
